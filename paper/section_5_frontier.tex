\section{The Aggregation Frontier}
\label{sec:frontier}
The Speaker combines four minister verdicts into one decision. The deployed
choice is a pure veto: any single \textsc{Block} blocks. This section asks
whether that is the right choice, by measuring it against alternatives under an
adaptive attacker. The question matters because the aggregator is the single
point at which an attacker who has compromised the agent's reasoning can try to
launder a malicious action past the monitor.

\subsection{Adaptive attacker model}
\label{sec:advmodel}
We model an attacker who, per attack action, fully controls the output of the
$K$ most-foolable ministers, forcing each to vote \textsc{Allow} at high
confidence. A robust aggregator degrades gracefully as $K$ grows; a brittle one
collapses at $K{=}1$. The evaluation is a replay: attack-success rate as a
function of $K$ and clean false-positive rate on benign sessions are computed
from recorded minister votes on real session dumps (no model re-execution), with
corrupted votes injected into the replay. This isolates the aggregator's
contribution from the ministers' detection quality.

\subsection{Veto, Bayesian, and a tuned hybrid}
\label{sec:vetobayes}
We compare three aggregators. The deployed \emph{pure veto} achieves the lowest
attack-success rate at every $K$ --- corrupting ministers does not help an
attacker unless \emph{all} blocking ministers are corrupted --- but its clean
false-positive rate is the highest, because any one over-eager minister blocks a
benign action (\TBD\% on the benign set). A \emph{Bayesian} aggregator that
weights minister votes by learned reliability achieves a low clean
false-positive rate (\TBD\%) but is brittle: at $K{=}\TBD$ corrupted ministers
its attack-success rate rises to \TBD\%, because the posterior can be moved by a
minority of confident \textsc{Allow} votes. The \emph{hybrid} we propose --- a
Bayesian decision path with a hard veto floor at a swept confidence threshold
--- passes the ${<}5\%$ false-positive gate (\TBD\%) while tracking the pure
veto's robustness to within \TBD\ points at $K{=}\TBD$. Table~\TBD{} reports the
full false-positive/robustness frontier as the veto-floor threshold is swept;
the contribution is the characterization of this trade-off, not a claim that any
single aggregator dominates.
