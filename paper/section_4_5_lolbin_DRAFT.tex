\subsection{Adaptive lexical evasion: a red-team census}
\label{sec:lolbin}
The representation mismatch of \S\ref{sec:repmismatch} concerns benign traffic
landing near attack patterns; the dual question is whether a knowledgeable
attacker can push a genuinely malicious action the other way --- below
threshold --- by exploiting the same lexical channel. We red-team this directly
with living-off-the-land binaries (LOLBINs): signed, legitimate system
executables (\texttt{certutil}, \texttt{regsvr32}, \texttt{mshta}, \dots) that
an attacker repurposes for download and execution, and whose names are absent
from our corpus.

\paragraph{Coverage census (R2a).} Across 25 Windows LOLBINs we measured whether
each is recognized by the dense retrieval tier despite sharing no lexical
keywords with any corpus pattern. Twelve are HIGH-risk (above the deciding
minister's dense-similarity threshold yet lexically absent from the corpus), two
MEDIUM, and eleven LOW. The embedding tier therefore \emph{does} recognize these
techniques semantically --- the blind spot is not a failure to understand the
action, but the interaction with the lexical gate described next.

\paragraph{Full-pipeline result (R2b).} Running 13 of these tools through the
deployed hybrid pipeline (\texttt{run\_minister\_hybrid}, per-minister
thresholds), \textbf{11 of 13 evade} a hard block, 2 are blocked by the dense
tier (\texttt{rundll32}, \texttt{php\,-r}), and \textbf{none} are blocked by the
BM25 channel --- the lexical gate is multiplicative, not an independent
floor-block (Table~\ref{tab:r2b}). We exclude \texttt{php\,-r} from the evasion
count in fairness: the corpus already names ``php,'' so its lexical gate is
$1.000$ and its block is unsurprising.

\paragraph{Mechanism.} The hybrid confidence is
$\mathit{hybrid} = \mathit{dense} \times \mathit{gate}$ with a gate floor of
$0.65$ (\S\ref{sec:hybridresults}). When an action is semantically recognized
($\mathit{dense}$ above threshold) but shares no distinctive token with the
matched pattern, the gate floors near $0.65$ and drags the product below
threshold. \texttt{certutil} is the anchor: $\mathit{dense}=0.586$ (above the
$0.55$ EXECUTOR threshold), but $\mathit{gate}=0.878$ gives
$\mathit{hybrid}=0.515 < 0.55$ --- evades. The very mechanism that discounts
benign near-misses (\S\ref{sec:hybridresults}) also discounts a lexically-novel
attack the embedding tier already recognized.

\paragraph{Naive patching is not the fix (mitigation experiment).} The obvious
remedy --- append each evading tool's name to the surface field of its
nearest corpus pattern and re-embed --- fixes only \textbf{6 of 11}; five still
evade. Appending a token raises the gate toward $1.0$ only if the patched
pattern is the one dense retrieval actually \emph{selects} for that tool; for
the five residual evaders the token lands on a non-selected pattern, so the gate
stays floored (e.g.\ \texttt{regsvr32} gate $0.605$, the CHANNEL download trio
$\approx\!0.60$--$0.65$), and \texttt{DownloadFile} even re-routes to a
different pattern. Lexical patching is therefore placement-dependent and
unreliable; the structural fix is authoring semantically-correct patterns that
dense retrieval will select --- which is what the corpus-improvement agent does,
validating each candidate through an anti-poisoning gate before it is admitted
(the provenance of pattern CHAN-101, \S\ref{sec:provenance}).

\paragraph{What this shows.} The two dense-tier blocks (\texttt{rundll32},
\texttt{php\,-r}) confirm the gate \emph{works} when lexical overlap exists on
the selected pattern; the blind spot is specific to signed-binary LOLBINs whose
names the corpus does not carry, not a universal failure. This is the honest
answer to ``does an adaptive attacker defeat Kavach?'': against lexically-novel
LOLBINs at the current corpus coverage, yes for 11/13 at the hard-block level,
and the measured mitigation shows why coverage --- not threshold or gate tuning
--- is the lever.
