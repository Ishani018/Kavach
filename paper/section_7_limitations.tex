\paragraph{No head-to-head with ClawGuard on AgentDojo.} ClawGuard
\cite{clawguard2026} achieves 0\% attack-success rate on AgentDojo under
explicit user-authored rules, which Kavach does not currently match. Kavach's
contribution is complementary --- semantic, embedding-based detection that
requires no per-deployment rule authoring and extends across session
trajectories --- but a fair evaluation should report both systems on the same
benchmark. A direct comparison is left to future work pending completion of our
AgentDojo run. We stress that all competitor figures we cite are the numbers
each paper reports for itself, not values we reproduced: ClawGuard's results are
from its own AgentDojo and MCPSafeBench evaluation (its public implementation
intercepts live OpenClaw tool-call sessions and ships no dataset-replay harness,
so it cannot be run against InjecAgent's static cases without a bespoke
adapter), and OpenClaw PRISM's~\cite{prism2026} figures are from its own
110-case corpus (we could not locate a public implementation). Neither system
reports InjecAgent strict/loose recall or false-positive rate, so any comparison
against Kavach's InjecAgent numbers is benchmark-mismatched; we therefore treat
these as reported-not-reproduced and defer a controlled head-to-head to future
work.

\paragraph{Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof.} The hash-chained Ledger detects
post-hoc edits to any recorded decision or its provenance, but an attacker with
write access to the database can recompute the entire chain. Defeating that
requires anchoring the chain head to an external append-only medium, which we do
not implement.

\paragraph{Provenance precision is corpus-bound.} All 401 corpus patterns
declare a specific source technique, so verdict provenance resolves to a named
MITRE ATT\&CK / ATLAS technique or OWASP category for every match rather than to
a per-minister default; the audit trail still records which basis was used. The
residual limitation is that provenance is only as precise as the corpus tagging
itself: a verdict inherits the technique attribution of the pattern it matched,
so a mis-tagged pattern would propagate a mis-attribution into the chain. This
is a corpus-quality concern, not a coverage gap.

\paragraph{Curated corpus.} The attack-pattern corpus is authored from published
taxonomies; novel techniques absent from those taxonomies will not be matched.
This is intrinsic to corpus-based semantic detection and motivates the live
knowledge-base ingestion we sketch as future work.

\paragraph{Small distinct-$N$, FPR driver, and an escalate-only minister.}
Our primary numbers come from InjecAgent's 62 distinct attacker tools
(30 direct-harm, 32 data-stealing) replayed across 17 user tasks; the
distinct-attack count is modest, so per-minister and per-threshold figures
should be read as indicative rather than tightly estimated. Two structural
findings from the run: the benign false-positive rate is driven almost entirely
by the CHANNEL minister --- a small number of over-broad exfiltration patterns
account for every benign hard-block --- which a corpus-quality pass should
target first; and VAULT's per-minister block threshold (0.75) sits above its
observed attack-confidence ceiling on this benchmark, so VAULT contributes
escalations rather than hard blocks. Inter-minister error correlation is low on
average ($\bar\rho=0.09$) but not uniform: the NAVIGATOR--CHANNEL pair reaches
$\rho=0.52$, reflecting overlapping pattern coverage that the aggregation
analysis (\S\ref{sec:frontier}) accounts for. A hand-authored probe of ten
unambiguously benign tool calls (\S\ref{sec:repmismatch}) --- hard-blocked five
of ten at the deployed thresholds --- is additional, benchmark-independent
evidence that the operating point, not the benign population, drives the
false-positive rate. Recalibrating per-minister thresholds and deduplicating the
CHANNEL/VAULT corpora are concrete next steps.

\paragraph{Single-vector similarity and the trajectory leg.} Per-call detection
uses single-vector cosine similarity, which has known limits relative to
sequence-aware detection; Trajectory Guard \cite{advani2026trajectoryguard}
reports that mean-pooling centroid approaches cap at F1 $\leq 0.69$ on
trajectory detection. Kavach's trajectory monitor uses lightweight context
signals rather than a trained sequence model, and the session-risk ceiling
requires intent seeding to reach full sensitivity. The session ceiling is
currently fixed at an empirically calibrated value (0.50, set just above the
observed benign session peak across ten multi-step sessions); modulating it by
COMPASS intent drift is a natural extension --- the architecture already supports
it since \texttt{compass\_sim} is in scope at the ceiling check --- but requires
drift-seeded calibration data we leave to future work. A learned, stage-aware
trajectory model over the provenance stage axis (\S\ref{sec:provenance}) is the
natural next step.

\paragraph{COMPASS intent-seed trust assumption.} COMPASS's drift signal depends
on a trustworthy intent vector extracted from the first non-system turn. An
attacker who controls that turn can supply a malicious seed aligned with the
intended attack trajectory, causing subsequent malicious actions to appear
semantically aligned rather than drifted --- effectively inverting the oracle
into a false-safety signal. Intent-seed validation --- for example, checking the
seed against a known-benign topic model or requiring a trusted out-of-band seed
channel --- is a direct mitigation we leave to future work.

\paragraph{Single backbone and runtime.} We evaluate with one agent backbone
(Gemma\,4~26B) on OpenClaw in embedded mode, without MXC. Cross-model generality
and a defense-in-depth evaluation inside an MXC sandbox --- the production
configuration implied by the Build~2026 Microsoft/OpenClaw partnership --- are
future work.

\paragraph{Single embedding model.} All detection uses a single encoder
(\texttt{bge-base-en-v1.5}), selected for open-source offline reproducibility
rather than by a comparative ablation; whether an alternative open-source
retrieval encoder would shift the recall/false-positive trade-off is an open
question we leave to future work.

\paragraph{Benchmark saturation.} InjecAgent and AgentDojo are increasingly
saturated on frontier models, which compresses the headroom in which a monitor's
benefit is visible. Multi-step staged attacks, which our trajectory monitor
targets, are under-represented in current benchmarks and motivate a purpose-built
scenario set --- itself designed to avoid contaminating the corpus.
