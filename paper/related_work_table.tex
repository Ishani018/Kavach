\noindent\small
\footnotetext{We do not report a single AgentDojo ASR figure for Kavach: our
AgentDojo evaluation (\S\ref{sec:channel-eval}, \S\ref{sec:gpt4o}) reports
per-mechanism detection rates (5/5 on structurally applicable ground-truth
cases; 74.2\% block-or-escalate on all 629 real recorded GPT-4o attack
trajectories, with every apparent miss traced to its actual cause) rather
than a single aggregate rate comparable to the other rows' self-reported
numbers, which are each computed under a different protocol.}
\textbf{Veto verdict} (Kavach): any single minister returning \textsc{Block}
vetoes the call unconditionally; no score averaging or learned aggregation.
\textbf{Corpus extensibility} (Kavach): deterministic prefilters are extended by
adding a new structural rule; the cosine-triage corpus is extended by adding a
natural-language attack-pattern description, re-embedded without retraining
any model component.
\textbf{Red-team / scanning tools} are complementary, not competing: garak
\cite{garak2024} (NVIDIA) and PyRIT \cite{pyrit2024} (Microsoft) are
\emph{offensive} test harnesses that probe a model/agent for vulnerabilities
pre-deployment rather than enforce at runtime, and LLM-Guard \cite{llmguard2024}
is an input/output scanner suite operating at the prompt boundary rather than the
tool-call boundary. Kavach is orthogonal: a runtime, tool-call-time enforcement
layer that these tools can be used to red-team.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Plan-time authorization (PlanGuard, IntentGuard, Progent).}
PlanGuard (arXiv:2604.10134) constructs an isolated plan from the user's
instructions alone, then gates execution with a hard-constraint stage and an
LLM-based intent verifier, reporting 100\% attack-blocking on InjecAgent.
Kavach's NAVIGATOR minister pins a session's user intent once, immutably,
analogously to PlanGuard's stage 1, and reduces authorization to a
deterministic question specifically to keep the enforcement decision
model-free --- but, unlike PlanGuard, we report this hard-constraint layer as
an incomplete, three-attempt negative result (\S\ref{par:navigator-authflow})
rather than a working mechanism: PlanGuard's LLM-based verifier may be doing
real work that our deterministic heuristics did not find a way to replace.
IntentGuard (arXiv:2512.00966) performs instruction-following intent analysis
with origin tracing and treats user-confirmation-on-alert as a first-class
mechanism rather than a fallback; this framing is close in spirit to how
CHANNEL's provenance mechanism deliberately escalates rather than blocks on
genuine ambiguity (\S\ref{sec:channel}), though IntentGuard applies it at the
plan level and Kavach at the argument-provenance level.
Progent (arXiv:2504.11703) enforces a progressive, per-tool allowlist policy
and is the closest external precedent for a policy-authorization layer,
reporting an AgentDojo ASR reduction from 39.9\% to 1.0\%; we cite this as an
upper reference point for what a mature policy layer can achieve, not as a
number Kavach reproduces, since Progent's policy-synthesis mechanism and
Kavach's deterministic-primary mechanism are not directly comparable.
